# Abstract

**Classification of Selected Skin Diseases Using Artificial Intelligence for Dark Skin Tones**

Paul Ajadi & Nancy Woods  
University of Ibadan

Artificial Intelligence has become an innovative technology with significant potential to address pressing challenges across diverse sectors, notably within the healthcare sector. Skin diseases pose a significant public health burden globally, with a particularly high impact in Africa due to various socio-economic factors. Early and accurate diagnosis is crucial for effective treatment and management. However, limited access to dermatologists and specialized healthcare services in many African regions leads to delayed diagnosis and treatment, exacerbating the burden of skin diseases.

This research developed a Convolutional Neural Network model for multi-class classification of skin diseases, targeting acne, eczema, and keloids in dark skin tones. A primary dataset was collected from dermatological centers, preprocessed, and augmented to enhance image quality and improve model generalization. The dataset comprised 400 instances of selected skin disease images which was augmented to 2,650 images by applying several image transformation techniques. Images were annotated with relevant metadata, such as disease labels, lesion boundaries, and clinical information.

The developed CNN model correctly identified 73%, 59% and 40% of acne, eczema and keloid cases respectively, achieving an overall accuracy of 57%, being a first attempt at using local data for such research. Qualitative analysis demonstrated its capability to classify selected skin diseases, providing reliable probability distributions across multiple skin conditions.

**Keywords:** Deep Learning, Convolutional Neural Networks, Dark skin tones, Transfer Learning, Skin Diseases, Acne, Keloids, Eczema
